# Mental Health Stigma — What It Is and Why It Matters

Stigma is one of the most significant barriers to students seeking mental health support. Understanding it helps dismantle it — in yourself and in how you talk to others.

## What Is Stigma?

Stigma is negative attitudes, beliefs, and behaviors directed at people with mental health conditions. It takes two forms:

- **Public stigma:** Societal stereotypes (e.g., people with mental health conditions are dangerous, weak, or unpredictable)
- **Self-stigma:** Internalizing those stereotypes about yourself ("I should be able to handle this on my own")

## How Stigma Affects Students

Research consistently shows stigma is the primary reason students delay seeking help — often by years. Students report:
- Fear of being judged by peers
- Concern that seeking help signals weakness
- Worry about it affecting academic or professional opportunities
- Cultural contexts in which mental health help-seeking is particularly stigmatized

## The Reality

- Seeking help is a sign of self-awareness and strength, not weakness
- Mental health conditions are medical — they respond to treatment
- Counseling records are confidential and separate from academic records
- Employers do not have access to your counseling history

## What You Can Do

- **Use person-first language:** "a student experiencing depression" not "a depressed student"
- **Challenge generalizations** when you hear them
- **Talk openly** about mental health (to the degree you are comfortable) — every person who shares their experience reduces stigma for others
- **Reach out** to the Active Minds chapter at your campus to get involved in awareness work
