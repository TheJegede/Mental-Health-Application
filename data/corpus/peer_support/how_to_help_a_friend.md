# How to Help a Friend Who Is Struggling

Noticing that a friend is struggling can feel overwhelming — especially if you are not sure what to say or whether you will make things worse. The research is clear: asking someone if they are okay, and meaning it, helps. It does not plant ideas or make things worse.

## Signs a Friend May Need Support

- Withdrawing from social activities they used to enjoy
- Talking about hopelessness, worthlessness, or being a burden
- Significant changes in sleep, appetite, or appearance
- Increased alcohol or drug use
- Giving away valued possessions
- Saying things like "I won't be a problem much longer" or "everyone would be better off without me"

## How to Start the Conversation

Choose a private, calm moment. You don't need a perfect script:
- "I've noticed you seem like you're going through something. I'm here if you want to talk."
- "I care about you and wanted to check in. How are you actually doing?"
- "I'm not going to judge you. I just want to understand."

## What to Do (and Not Do)

**Do:**
- Listen without trying to fix or minimizing ("I hear you, that sounds really hard")
- Stay present — you don't have to have answers
- Ask directly if you're concerned: "Are you thinking about hurting yourself?"
- Encourage them to seek professional support and offer to help them find it

**Don't:**
- Promise to keep it a secret (you may need to involve others if safety is at risk)
- Dismiss or compare ("everyone feels that way sometimes")
- Leave them alone if you believe they are in immediate danger

## After the Conversation

Check in again. A single conversation matters, and follow-through matters more. "I wanted to see how you're doing since we talked" goes a long way.
