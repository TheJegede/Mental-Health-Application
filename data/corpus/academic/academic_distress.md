# When Academic Stress Becomes More Than Stress

Academic pressure is a normal part of college. But there is a difference between situational stress and distress that is affecting your health and daily functioning.

## Normal Academic Stress vs. Concerning Patterns

| Normal stress | Signs to take seriously |
|---|---|
| Temporary anxiety before exams | Persistent anxiety that doesn't go away after the exam |
| Difficulty sleeping during finals week | Weeks of disrupted sleep regardless of workload |
| Feeling overwhelmed at the start of a new quarter | Sustained feeling that you cannot cope |
| Occasional low motivation | Extended inability to do basic tasks |
| Worry about grades | Thoughts that everything is hopeless or that you don't belong at UCLA |

## Common Triggers for Students

- Imposter syndrome (feeling like you don't belong or will be "found out")
- First-generation student pressure
- Financial stress layered onto academic demands
- Major life changes (moving away from home, relationship changes, loss)
- Underlying conditions that college transitions sometimes surface

## What You Can Do

- **Talk to someone:** CAPS Let's Talk drop-ins are free and require no appointment
- **Reach out to your TA or professor:** Academic struggles are easier to address early; most faculty want to help
- **Use CAE:** If mental health symptoms are affecting academics, academic accommodations may help
- **Name what you're experiencing:** "I'm struggling" is a complete sentence and a valid starting point

## When to Seek Help Immediately

If academic stress has escalated to thoughts of self-harm or suicide, contact CAPS immediately at (310) 825-0768 or call/text 988.
