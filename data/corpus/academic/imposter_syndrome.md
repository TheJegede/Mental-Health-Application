# Imposter Syndrome in College

Imposter syndrome is the persistent feeling that you do not deserve your success, that you were admitted by mistake, or that you will soon be "found out" as incompetent — despite evidence of your actual ability.

## How Common Is It?

Studies estimate 70%+ of people experience imposter syndrome at some point. It is especially common in high-achieving students, first-generation students, students from underrepresented backgrounds, and those in highly competitive programs.

## Why It Feels So Real

The experience of imposter syndrome is genuine — the distress is real, even when the underlying belief is not accurate. It is not a character flaw or evidence of actual incompetence.

## Common Patterns

- Attributing successes to luck, timing, or others' low standards
- Believing you have "fooled" admissions, instructors, or employers
- Discounting positive feedback while amplifying negative
- Setting impossibly high standards, then dismissing achievement when you meet them

## Strategies That Help

- **Name it:** Recognizing "this is imposter syndrome" interrupts automatic belief
- **Track evidence:** Keep a record of actual achievements, completed work, and positive feedback
- **Talk to others:** Most people in your program feel this way and don't say so; finding out you're not alone is reliably helpful
- **Reframe failure:** Mistakes are part of learning, not proof of incompetence

## For First-Generation and Underrepresented Students

Imposter syndrome is often more intense when you lack family members who attended the same type of institution. CAPS group therapy includes groups specifically for first-gen students and for students navigating identity and belonging.
