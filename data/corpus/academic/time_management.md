# Time Management and Mental Health

Chronic time pressure and overwhelm are among the most common complaints at CAPS. This is not laziness or poor character — it is usually the result of a genuinely overloaded schedule meeting an undertrained skill.

## Why Time Management Affects Mental Health

- Constant backlog creates persistent low-grade anxiety
- Deadline emergencies produce acute stress that damages sleep and recovery
- Feeling out of control of your schedule feeds helplessness
- Procrastination is often anxiety avoidance, not laziness — and creates a shame spiral

## Evidence-Based Strategies

### Time blocking
Assign specific tasks to specific time slots rather than working from a general to-do list. "Study for bio exam" is not actionable; "review Chapter 7 notes, 2–3pm Thursday" is.

### Parkinson's Law
Work expands to fill available time. Setting a shorter deadline with a clear stopping point often produces the same output with less time spent.

### The 2-minute rule
If a task takes less than 2 minutes, do it now rather than scheduling it. Deferred small tasks accumulate into a cognitive load that exceeds their actual demands.

### Weekly review
Every Sunday, spend 20 minutes reviewing the coming week: what is due, what preparations are needed, and where there is realistic time to do them.

## When Overwhelm Becomes More

If organizational strategies are not helping and you are experiencing persistent anxiety, depression, or difficulty functioning, CAPS counselors can help assess whether there is an underlying issue (like untreated ADHD or anxiety) that is making time management harder than it should be.
