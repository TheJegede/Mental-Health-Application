# Mental Health Support for First-Generation Students

First-generation college students — those whose parents did not complete a four-year college degree — face a unique intersection of pressures that can affect mental health.

## Stressors Specific to First-Gen Students

- Navigating institutional systems without family members who have done it before
- Carrying a sense of responsibility to succeed for family who sacrificed for the opportunity
- "Code-switching" between home culture and campus culture
- Financial stress at higher rates than continuing-generation students
- Imposter syndrome that can be particularly acute in unfamiliar academic environments
- Less family familiarity with mental health help-seeking

## You Belong Here

Being first-generation is not a disadvantage in terms of capability. Research shows first-gen students demonstrate high resilience and resourcefulness. The gap is in access to information and support systems, not potential.

## Services at UCLA

- **CAPS individual therapy:** No prior mental health experience needed; counselors understand first-gen experience
- **Group therapy for first-gen students:** Offered most terms — a space to process these specific challenges with peers
- **First to Go:** UCLA's first-generation student support program — community events, peer mentorship, and resources at [firsttogo.ucla.edu](https://www.firsttogo.ucla.edu/)

## Financial Access to Mental Health Support

CAPS services are covered by student fees for enrolled students. If you are considering seeing an outside therapist and cost is a barrier, CAPS can provide referrals to low-cost community mental health providers.
