# ADHD Support at UCLA

ADHD (Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder) is one of the most common neurodevelopmental conditions affecting college students. Many students are not diagnosed until college, when self-management demands increase significantly.

## Common Signs of Undiagnosed ADHD in College

- Difficulty sustaining attention on coursework even when motivated
- Starting tasks but rarely completing them before distraction
- Consistent underperformance relative to apparent ability and effort
- Difficulty with time estimation ("how is it already midnight?")
- Emotional dysregulation — frustration, rejection sensitivity
- May be inattentive-only (no hyperactivity) — often missed in students who "seemed fine" in structured high school environments

## Getting an Evaluation

1. **Contact CAPS:** (310) 825-0768 — discuss your concerns; CAPS can conduct screening and refer for formal evaluation
2. **Student Health:** UCLA Student Health offers ADHD evaluation and, if diagnosed, medication management
3. **CAE:** If you have a prior diagnosis or receive a new one, academic accommodations are available through the Center for Accessible Education

## Accommodations

Students with ADHD commonly receive:
- Extended time on exams (1.5× or 2×)
- Reduced-distraction testing environment
- Note-taking support

Contact CAE at (310) 825-1501 to apply.

## Treatment Options

- **Medication:** Stimulant and non-stimulant medications are effective; discuss with Student Health or a CAPS psychiatrist
- **Coaching and therapy:** ADHD coaching and CBT-based therapy address executive function strategies
- **Accommodations:** Environmental modifications reduce performance gaps

## Note on Medication

CAPS psychiatrists evaluate for ADHD medication. Stimulant medications require documentation of diagnosis and follow-up appointments. Do not use someone else's medication — it is illegal and can be unsafe.
