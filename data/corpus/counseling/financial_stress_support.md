# Financial Stress and Mental Health at UCLA

Financial stress is one of the most significant, and least-discussed, mental health stressors for college students. ACHA-NCHA data consistently shows ~30% of students report that finances have negatively affected their academic performance.

## How Financial Stress Affects Mental Health

- Persistent worry that does not resolve between study sessions
- Difficulty concentrating due to background financial anxiety
- Sleep disruption
- Shame that prevents students from seeking help
- Compounding effect: financial stress makes other stressors harder to manage

## You Are Not Alone and You Are Not the Problem

Financial struggle in college is structural, not a personal failing. First-generation students, Pell Grant recipients, and students from low-income families face this at higher rates — and are often the students least likely to ask for help because of stigma.

## Campus Resources for Financial Support

- **Financial Aid Office:** If your financial situation has changed, you may qualify for an appeal or emergency aid. Contact the Financial Aid Office: [financialaid.ucla.edu](https://www.financialaid.ucla.edu/)
- **Bruin Basic Needs Hub:** Addresses food insecurity, housing instability, and financial emergencies — [basicneeds.ucla.edu](https://basicneeds.ucla.edu/)
- **Emergency Funds:** UCLA has emergency fund programs for students facing unexpected financial hardship. Ask at the Financial Aid Office or through your Dean of Students office.

## Talking to CAPS About Financial Stress

CAPS counselors regularly support students navigating financial stress. You do not need to separate "academic/financial stress" from "mental health" — they are connected, and counselors can help you address both.
