# Confidentiality at UCLA CAPS

Everything you share with a CAPS counselor is confidential. This is a legal and ethical requirement — your counselor cannot share your information with your parents, professors, university administrators, or anyone else without your written permission.

## What Confidentiality Means

- Your counseling records are separate from academic records
- Your parents or family cannot be notified that you are receiving services
- Faculty and staff are not informed about your mental health status

## Exceptions to Confidentiality

There are narrow, legally defined exceptions:

1. **Imminent danger to self or others:** If your counselor believes you are at serious, imminent risk of harming yourself or another person, they may contact emergency services or a designated person.
2. **Child, elder, or dependent adult abuse:** Counselors are mandated reporters and must report known or suspected abuse.
3. **Valid court order:** If a court legally requires it.
4. **With your written consent:** You can sign a release form to allow your counselor to communicate with specific people (a parent, a doctor, another therapist).

## Talking to Your Counselor About Sensitive Topics

Because of these protections, you can speak openly about:
- Substance use
- Sexual behavior
- Mental health symptoms
- Things you have not told family or friends

The goal is to give you a safe space to get the support you need.

## Questions?

If you have questions about confidentiality before or during a session, ask your counselor directly. They are required to explain the limits of confidentiality at the start of treatment.
