# Eating Disorders — Overview and Support

Eating disorders are serious mental health conditions with the highest mortality rate of any psychiatric diagnosis. They affect students of all genders, backgrounds, and body sizes.

## Types of Eating Disorders

- **Anorexia Nervosa:** Restriction of food intake, intense fear of weight gain, distorted body image
- **Bulimia Nervosa:** Cycles of binge eating followed by purging (vomiting, laxative use, over-exercise)
- **Binge Eating Disorder:** Recurrent episodes of eating large amounts in a short time, with feelings of loss of control and significant distress
- **ARFID (Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder):** Avoidance of foods based on sensory characteristics or fear of negative consequences — not driven by body image
- **OSFED:** Eating disorder presentations that cause significant distress but don't meet full criteria for the above

## Why College Is a High-Risk Period

- Transition from family food structures to self-managed eating
- Heightened social comparison and appearance pressure
- Academic stress acting as a trigger
- First time living independently (less external monitoring)
- Increased alcohol use interacting with food-related behaviors

- **Crisis Text Line:** Text NEDA to 741741 (24/7)
- **UCLA CAPS:** (310) 825-0768 — individual therapy and referral to eating disorder specialists

## What to Do If You Are Concerned About Yourself or a Friend

Early intervention significantly improves outcomes. If you or someone you know is showing signs of an eating disorder:
1. Talk to a CAPS counselor — they can assess and refer to specialists
2. Contact the National Alliance for Eating Disorders helpline for guidance
3. For medical emergencies (fainting, severe physical symptoms), call 911

Recovery is possible. You do not have to be "sick enough" to deserve support.
