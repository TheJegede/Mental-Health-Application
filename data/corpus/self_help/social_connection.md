# Social Connection and Loneliness in College

Loneliness is one of the most common experiences among college students — and one of the least talked about. Research from the Healthy Minds Study consistently shows that 60%+ of college students report significant loneliness in a given year.

## Why College Can Feel Isolating

- Large campus: easy to be surrounded by people and still feel alone
- Social media creates a misleading impression that everyone else is thriving
- Transition from existing friendships takes time to rebuild
- Academic demands leave little time for unplanned social interaction
- First-generation students and international students face additional adjustment layers

## Loneliness vs. Introversion

Loneliness is the gap between the social connection you want and what you have — it is about quality, not just quantity. An introvert can be socially satisfied with fewer connections; loneliness occurs when that need isn't met regardless of how social someone "should" be.

## Strategies for Building Connection

- **Join one thing:** A single consistent activity (club, intramural sport, study group) provides repeated contact with the same people — the precursor to actual friendship
- **Be the initiator:** Most students are waiting for someone else to reach out; the person who initiates is often welcomed
- **Use existing proximity:** Residence hall, study spaces, and recurring classes provide natural contact opportunities
- **Quality over quantity:** One genuine conversation matters more than dozens of surface interactions

## When to Seek Support

If loneliness has been persistent for several weeks and is affecting your mood, motivation, or wellbeing, talking to a counselor can help. Group therapy at CAPS is specifically effective for connection-related concerns.
