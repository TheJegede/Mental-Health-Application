# Understanding and Managing Depression — Student Guide

Depression is more than feeling sad. It is a condition that affects how you feel, think, and function — and it is one of the most common health concerns among college students.

## What Depression Can Look Like in Students

- Persistent low mood, emptiness, or irritability
- Loss of interest in activities you used to enjoy
- Fatigue and low energy even after adequate sleep
- Difficulty concentrating, making decisions, or remembering things
- Changes in appetite (eating much more or much less)
- Withdrawing from friends, classes, or activities
- Feeling worthless, guilty, or like a burden
- In severe cases: thoughts of death or suicide

Not everyone experiences all of these. Depression looks different for different people.

## Why College Is a Common Onset Point

Many students experience their first significant depressive episode in college. Contributing factors include:
- Leaving the support systems of home and family
- Academic pressure and performance demands
- Social adjustment and loneliness
- Sleep disruption and irregular eating
- First time managing mental health without parental scaffolding

## What Helps

- **Professional support:** Therapy (particularly CBT and behavioral activation) and/or medication are effective treatments. Contact CAPS: (310) 825-0768
- **Behavioral activation:** Depression reduces motivation to do things; doing things (even small ones) is a powerful antidote. Schedule one small activity per day, even if you don't "feel like it"
- **Social connection:** Isolation feeds depression. Maintaining at least one social contact per week — even briefly — helps
- **Sleep and exercise:** Both have strong evidence as depression management tools

## If You Are Having Thoughts of Suicide

This is a medical urgency. Please reach out now:
- Call or text **988** (24/7)
- Text **HOME to 741741** (24/7)
- CAPS: (310) 825-0768
- Campus emergency: (310) 825-1491
