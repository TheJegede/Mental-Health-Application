# Sleep and Mental Health

Sleep disruption is one of the most reliable early indicators of mental health difficulties. Poor sleep worsens anxiety, depression, and cognitive functioning — and mental health challenges worsen sleep. Both directions of this relationship are real.

## How Much Sleep Do Students Need?

Most college-aged adults need 7–9 hours per night. Consistently getting less than 6 hours impairs memory consolidation, mood regulation, and immune function.

## Common Sleep Problems in Students

- Difficulty falling asleep due to racing thoughts or anxiety
- Waking in the early hours and being unable to return to sleep
- Oversleeping as a depression symptom (hypersomnia)
- Delayed sleep phase (natural night-owl pattern exaggerated by campus schedules)
- Sleep disrupted by alcohol use

## Evidence-Based Sleep Hygiene

1. **Consistent schedule:** Wake up at the same time every day, including weekends — this anchors your circadian rhythm
2. **Light exposure:** Get bright light in the morning; reduce screen brightness 1–2 hours before bed
3. **Reserve the bed for sleep:** Working in bed trains your brain to associate the space with wakefulness
4. **Limit caffeine after 2pm:** Half-life of caffeine is 5–6 hours
5. **Wind-down routine:** 20–30 minutes of low-stimulation activity before bed

## When to Seek Professional Help

See a CAPS counselor or student health provider if:
- Sleep problems have persisted for more than 2 weeks
- Daytime functioning is significantly impaired
- You are using alcohol or substances to sleep
