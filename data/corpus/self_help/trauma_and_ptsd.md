# Trauma and PTSD — Support Resources

Trauma is not just about what happened — it is about how the nervous system was affected. Traumatic experiences can include assault, accidents, natural disasters, childhood abuse, sudden loss, or witnessing violence.

## Trauma Responses Are Normal Reactions

Common responses after traumatic events:
- Intrusive memories, flashbacks, or nightmares
- Hypervigilance (feeling "on edge," difficulty relaxing)
- Avoidance of reminders of the event
- Emotional numbness or disconnection
- Difficulty trusting others

These are normal responses to abnormal events. Having them does not mean something is permanently wrong with you.

## PTSD vs. Normal Trauma Response

Many trauma responses resolve within weeks to months with support. PTSD is diagnosed when symptoms persist for more than a month and significantly impair functioning. PTSD is treatable — evidence-based therapies (EMDR, CPT, Prolonged Exposure) have strong research support.

## UCLA CAPS Trauma Services

- CAPS provides trauma-informed therapy for enrolled students
- Referrals to trauma specialists in the community for cases requiring specialized treatment
- Sexual assault resources: UCLA Title IX Office and CARE Advocate program (confidential)

## CARE Program (UCLA)

For students who have experienced sexual violence, relationship violence, or stalking:
- **CARE Advocate:** Confidential advocacy and support — [care.ucla.edu](https://www.care.ucla.edu/)
- **Phone:** (310) 206-2542
- Available to all genders

## Immediate Crisis Support

If you are in immediate danger or experiencing a mental health crisis related to trauma:
- Call 911 or (310) 825-1491 (UCLA campus emergency)
- Call or text 988 (24/7)
- CAPS crisis line: (310) 825-0768 (after-hours option available)
