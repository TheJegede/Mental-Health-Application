# Grief and Loss — Support for Students

Grief is the natural response to loss — and college years are often when students first encounter significant losses: a grandparent, a parent, a friend, a relationship, or a sense of the life they thought they would have.

## Grief Is Not Linear

The old "five stages" model (denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance) is a myth of linear progression. Grief moves in waves and often resurfaces unexpectedly — at the start of a new semester, on anniversaries, or during stress.

## Types of Loss That Cause Grief

- Death of a loved one
- End of a long-term relationship
- Loss of a home, community, or country (particularly for international and first-gen students)
- Loss of a prior identity or belief system
- Loss of academic trajectory (e.g., not getting into a program you planned for)
- Anticipatory grief (a loved one is ill; a relationship is ending)

## What Grief in College Looks Like

- Difficulty concentrating when grief resurfaces
- Irritability that seems disconnected from immediate events
- Feeling like you are "over it" and then being overwhelmed again
- Guilt about laughing or enjoying things
- Pressure to appear okay academically when you are not

## Getting Support

- UCLA CAPS offers a grief and loss support group most terms: (310) 825-0768
- Individual therapy for grief is available at CAPS
- Academic accommodations can be arranged through CAE if grief is affecting academic performance

## When to Seek Help Urgently

If grief has led to thoughts of not wanting to be alive or harming yourself, please contact CAPS or call/text 988 now. Grief that reaches this level needs and deserves immediate support.
