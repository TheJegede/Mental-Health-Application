# Managing Anxiety — Self-Help Strategies

Anxiety is the most common mental health concern among college students. Some level of anxiety is normal and adaptive. When it becomes persistent, intrusive, or disabling, it benefits from attention.

## What Anxiety Feels Like

- Physical: racing heart, tight chest, shallow breathing, muscle tension, nausea, sweating
- Cognitive: racing or looping thoughts, worst-case thinking, difficulty concentrating
- Behavioral: avoidance of situations that trigger anxiety, procrastination, reassurance-seeking

## Strategies That Work

### Cognitive reframing
Anxious thoughts often overestimate threat and underestimate your ability to cope. Ask:
- "What is the actual evidence for this?"
- "What is the realistic worst case, and could I handle it?"
- "Am I treating a feeling as a fact?"

### Scheduled worry time
If anxiety is pervasive, try confining it. Pick a 15-minute window each day as your "worry time." When anxious thoughts arise outside that window, defer them: "I'll think about that at 5pm." This reduces the sense that you must address every anxious thought immediately.

### Exposure
Avoiding anxiety-provoking situations makes the anxiety stronger over time. Gradual, supported exposure is one of the most effective anxiety treatments. A CAPS therapist can help structure this.

### Physical activity
Regular aerobic exercise reliably reduces anxiety symptoms. Even 20 minutes of brisk walking has measurable effects.

## When Self-Help Is Not Enough

If anxiety is significantly affecting your academic performance, relationships, or quality of life, CAPS can help. Effective treatments exist — you do not have to manage this alone.
